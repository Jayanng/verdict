The court never touches the money.

A freelancer and a client lock money in an Ootle escrow. The deal goes wrong, the client goes silent, or the freelancer disappears. Either side opens a dispute, three bonded strangers rule it, and the escrow's own code moves its own vault. The court issues the ruling and nothing else.

Eight rules the court cannot break.

The invariants, in plain language. Each one is checked by engine tests in the repo.

01

The vault moves only inside the escrow

Only on a ruling delivered through execute_ruling, and only when the court calls it. Never on a single vote, never by the court directly.

02

One vote per drawn juror

A juror votes at most once per dispute, and only if drawn for it.

03

Every path conserves micro-Tari

Settlement never pays out more than the escrow holds. Dust is resolved by largest remainder.

04

A ruling executes at most once

Settle once, and the second call is refused by the court and by the escrow.

05

Evidence cannot change

An evidence hash, once posted, cannot be changed.

06

The draw is recomputable

Anyone can re-derive the panel from the published inputs: future epoch hash, pool list, dispute id, round.

07

No owner anywhere

No owner, no admin method, no upgrade, on the court or either escrow. Ownerlessness is what makes the caller rule absolute.

08

The court can always be exited

Ruling, appeal, or bounded no-ruling fallback. Money is never frozen forever.

Honest limits.

What Verdict does not do, stated on the page, not hidden.

The court never touches the money

The escrow's own ownerless code executes rulings. The caller rule is readable on chain.

Two of three jurors can collude

The unknowable future-epoch draw, bonds, commit-reveal, and the appeal are the mitigations. In a small pool the panel is known after the draw.

Evidence is public and off chain

Hashes bind content, availability is not guaranteed. Jurors may refuse to arbitrate.

No audit

Engine tests plus simulation parity tests are the verification standard.

The court can always be exited

It never holds money hostage.

How it works.

Six steps, from a stuck deal to a ruling the escrow executes.

1

Open the dispute

Either side opens it. The escrow freezes its own vault, and the draw locks to a future epoch.

2

Post the evidence

Up to 3 items per side, each with a SHA-256 hash, a location, and a byte length. Posted means posted.

3

Draw the jury

In the locked epoch only, three bonded strangers are drawn from the frozen pool by the future epoch hash.

4

Commit, then reveal

Each juror commits a hash of their vote and salt, then reveals. A juror who never reveals is replaced.

5

The majority rules

Pay, refund, or split. A tie goes to appeal with seven jurors, funded by the appellant.

6

The escrow executes

The court calls execute_ruling, the escrow moves its own vault, and the remainder goes to the payer. If quorum fails, the bounded no-ruling fallback closes the dispute and unfreezes the escrow.
